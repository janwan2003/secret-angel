# secret-angel (working name; weeks-long secret-friend game: anonymous notes and small kindnesses, guess your angel at the end)

## Thesis

**Who:** Office teams, school staff rooms, classes, uni halls and societies, church groups, youth
groups, sports clubs and big families. These groups already run a *multi-week* secret-friend
game under local names. In the Philippines it is Monito Monita (weekly themed tokens such as
"something red", "something sweet", ending in a Christmas-party reveal). In Ireland it is Kris
Kindle week. Singapore and Malaysia have Angels & Mortals (halls, orientation camps, companies).
In the US there are teachers' "Secret Pal" and churches' "Secret Sister". All of these are
ordinary people, typically organised by one person with a paper draw, a Google Form of
"favourite things" and, at most, a home-made Telegram bot.

**What:** A no-login secret-friend game in one group link. The organiser picks a length (1 week,
the 24 days of Advent, a month), a token budget and a theme pack (Monito-style weekly themes,
daily kindness missions, or none). Members join with a nickname and fill a 6-question "favourite
things" card. The draw gives each member a **mortal** to look after in secret; each member is
also someone else's mortal. During the game:
- the angel can send anonymous notes to their mortal through the app, and the mortal can reply
  to "my angel" without knowing who that is;
- each day or week brings a theme or a small mission ("leave a note on their desk", "something
  sweet");
- the mortal logs a guess ("I think it's Ana") whenever they like, and can change it;
- the group feed shows only counts ("23 notes sent today", "4 people think they've worked theirs
  out").
On reveal day (the Christmas party, the last day of term) the chain unmasks one link at a
time on a TV or on everyone's phone, showing who guessed right and the best anonymous note
(chosen by vote).

**Needs others:** yes, completely. You can't be your own angel, and the whole game is the
hidden ring of people.

**Viral moment:** the reveal ("I was sure it was Tom. It was the new intern."), the
"guessed it" leaderboard, and the full ring diagram of who had whom, screenshotted into other
chats.

**How often:** seasonal, with several triggers per year: Advent/Christmas (now), Valentine's
("Secret Cupid"), Teacher Appreciation Week, orientation and the start of term, Ramadan or Lent
kindness weeks, team onboarding. A typical member is in 2–4 such groups (work, family, church,
friends).

**Why now:** Christmas runs of Monito Monita and Kris Kindle start around 1 December, so the window
is 8 days away and closes on 24 December. Secret Santa tools (Elfster, DrawNames, our own
secret-santa-draw) only handle one gift. None of them runs the weeks of anonymous messages and
guessing that make these games fun.

### The six questions

1. **Why the group IS the input:** the draw needs everyone, and every note comes from a member
   and goes to a member. With one person there is no ring.
2. **Reveal moment:** the unmasking on the last day, link by link, with "guessed it ✓" marks and
   the "best note" award. During the game there are smaller ones: the first note from your angel,
   and each week's theme drop.
3. **Ritual or deadline:** a fixed length ending at a real event, such as the party, the last day
   of term or 24 December. Daily or weekly themes keep people coming back in between.
4. **30-second join:** chat link → nickname → 6 taps of favourite things (sweet/savoury,
   coffee/tea, ...) → "You're in. Draw happens when the organiser closes joining". No account and
   no email. A private return link is saved on the device, with an optional "copy my secret link"
   to guard against a lost phone.
5. **Screenshot-worthy result:** the ring diagram, everyone's "I guessed..." vs "it was..." card,
   and the anonymous note that won the vote.
6. **Why a joiner creates their own:** everyone in an office game is also in a family chat, a
   friend group or a church group. The reveal screen ends with "Run one for another group",
   with dates prefilled for the next trigger (Secret Cupid in February).

  (spending cap, identity guessed at the end). It appears in NTU hall events

  (example prefix "nhouse2022"). That is a demand signal: people code this themselves.

  [The Gifted Gabber](https://thegiftedgabber.com/secret-pal) (with monthly themes),
  [We Are Teachers](https://www.weareteachers.com/teachers-favorite-things-form/),

## Our edge

- **Loop: needs others.** The ring is the product.
- **K estimate ≈ 1.8** = 15 invited per game (office team, class, church group; Monito groups are
  often 10–30) × 80% join (the organiser's group expects to be in it) × 15% later run one for
  another circle (family, friends, Secret Cupid in February). Weakest factor: joiners becoming
  creators, because the game is seasonal. The prefilled "next trigger" date on the reveal screen
  is the fix.
- Beats the Telegram/Slack versions on reach (any chat app, no bot setup, no workspace). Beats
  Secret Santa tools on the actual game (weeks of notes, themes, guessing, a reveal).
- **Boundary:** secret-santa-draw (launched) already has anonymous questions to your giftee. The
  operator should decide whether secret-angel is a standalone product or a "multi-week mode" of
  secret-santa-draw. Recommendation: standalone, because the name, the reveal and the
  non-Christmas triggers are different. secret-missions (Killer) is a party game with targets,
  not kindness. kind-words is a one-shot compliment circle.
- **Risks:** anonymous messaging between coworkers or students can be misused. Countermeasures:
  the organiser sees a moderation view, the mortal can report a note (the organiser then sees the
  sender), there is a word filter, notes are text only, it is for 13+, and data is deleted 14
  days after the reveal.
